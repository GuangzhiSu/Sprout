The problem

Chances to practise are themselves scarce

01

The real world has no undo

A misread conversation or a meltdown at the till is paid for by the whole family — and there is no second take.

02

Support doesn't scale

Structured social training depends on therapists and role-play partners. Between sessions, the practice simply stops.

03

Every profile is different

Sound, eye contact, or simply not knowing how to open — one curriculum cannot hold all of these differences.

Capabilities

A rehearsal system you can control, observe and tune

Every capability is a switch. Start with text only, and add voice or facial cues when — and if — it helps.

👥

Agents with a personality

Cashier, classmate, teacher — each with their own goal, patience and mood curve. They follow up, get distracted, and can wait for the child's pace.

🎚️

Optional multimodal sensing

Pace and pauses in speech, subtle changes in expression — enabled only with a parent's consent, and used to adjust difficulty, never to score a child.

🩺

A clinician agent that steps in

Appears at emotional peaks and dips: a way out for the child, an explanation for the parent, and a suggestion to end the round when that's the right call.

📈

Multimodal tracking

Engagement, self-initiated turns, mood swings and exit moments become a timeline you can bring to a clinic — not a single score.

🧩

Personalised plans

Case notes and a parent's description shape the scenario sequence, with goals split into core and bonus.

🛟

An exit that's always there

Every scene offers “let me step back”. Leaving isn't failure — it's logged as an act of self-regulation.

Scenario library

From a checkout to an introduction

Scenarios are layered by how unavoidable they are: core scenes cover daily life, bonus scenes stretch the comfort zone.

🛒
CoreL1 – L3

Supermarket

Find the item, queue, answer the cashier, handle noise and waiting.

  • Core: finish one checkout exchange
  • Bonus: ask where something is
🎒
CoreL2 – L4

Classroom

Group up, take turns, and find the way back after an interruption.

  • Core: accept a role in the group
  • Bonus: offer your own idea
💬
BonusL3 – L5

A conversation

Start from a shared interest; practise opening, asking back, and ending gracefully.

  • Core: answer one question
  • Bonus: hand the topic back

In the works: waiting rooms, birthday parties, a first subway ride.

Tracking

We record states, not grades

Each round leaves an explainable trace: when the child offered one more sentence, when they needed to leave. Parents and clinicians read the same timeline.

  • Engagement and curiosity: who moves the conversation forward
  • Peaks and dips: what triggered them, how long recovery took
  • Case import: turn existing records into scenario parameters
  • Export: a one-page summary for the next appointment

Built with parents

Parents aren't spectators — they're co-designers

1

Describe the child

Write down triggers and interests in everyday words, or import an existing assessment.

2

Pick and rewrite a scene

Swap a line, turn the noise down, replace a stranger with a familiar neighbour.

3

Sit alongside

Pause, take over a role, or simply watch quietly.

4

Review and adjust

Read the timeline together and decide what — and how far — to practise next.
